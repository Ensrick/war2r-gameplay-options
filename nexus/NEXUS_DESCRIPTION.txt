[size=6][b]Gameplay Options for Warcraft II: Remastered[/b][/size]

One small DLL and one text file, [b]gameplay_options.toml[/b], that put the single-player game under your control: casters that use their spells on their own the way the computer's do, workers that go back to work, buildings that train by themselves, forests that grow back, and every unit number (health, prices, build times, damage, armor, range, sight, spell damage, mana) open to editing. Every feature has its own switch.

[b]Out of the box[/b] only four autocasts (Heal, Slow, Bloodlust, Raise Dead) and worker auto-repair are switched on. Everything else is off, every multiplier is 1.0, and the unit examples in the file are comments: nothing rebalances your game until you turn it on.

[b]Single-player only.[/b] The mod switches itself off in multiplayer games, and it changes no game file.

[b]What it can do[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Autocast[/b] for every spell in the game, each with its own switch
[*][b]Auto-production:[/b] idle buildings train workers and an army whose mix follows the map, your hall tier, your upgrades and your bank
[*][b]Idle workers[/b] that repair and go back to harvesting
[*][b]Forests that grow back[/b], never near your buildings and never closing a path
[*][b]Resources:[/b] richer gold mines and oil, halls that give food, unlimited mines if you want them
[*][b]Balance:[/b] health, prices, build and research times, spell damage and mana cost, per race, per group or per single unit
[*][b]Regeneration[/b] for heroes and, separately, for every unit
[/list]

[line]
[size=5][b]Installation[/b][/size]
[list=1]
[*]Close the game.
[*]Open the zip. It contains one folder, [b]x86[/b].
[*]Extract it into your [b]Warcraft II Remastered[/b] folder, the one that already contains an [b]x86[/b] folder. Default location: [i]C:\Program Files (x86)\Warcraft II Remastered[/i]. Say yes if Windows asks to merge folders.
[*]Check that these four files are now in [i]Warcraft II Remastered\x86[/i]: version.dll, gameplay_options.toml, gameplay_options_readme.txt, gameplay_options_tutorial.txt
[*]Start the game from Battle.net as usual.
[/list]
[b]Is it working?[/b] Start a single-player game and press [b]Ctrl+F9[/b]. A line of text "Autocast OFF" / "Autocast ON" appears in game. A file [i]x86\gameplay_options.log[/i] is also written on every start.

[b]Uninstall:[/b] delete version.dll and the gameplay_options files from the x86 folder.

[line]
[size=5][b]How to use gameplay_options.toml[/b][/size]
Open [i]x86\gameplay_options.toml[/i] with Notepad. It is split into numbered parts and every setting has a comment above it that says what it does.
[list]
[*][b]Save while you play.[/b] The mod re-reads the file every few seconds. "Gameplay Options: settings reloaded" appears in game. No restart.
[*][b]Mistakes are safe.[/b] After a typo the mod keeps your previous settings, tells you in game, and writes the line number to gameplay_options.log. A misspelled setting name is reported in the log as "unknown key".
[*][b]Delete to reset.[/b] Delete the file and a fresh one with the defaults is written the next time you start a game.
[/list]

[b]The format in one minute[/b]
[code]# a line starting with # is a comment

[spells]              # a section name in square brackets
heal = true           # on / off settings are true or false

[heal]
min_missing_hp = 10   # numbers are written plain

[general]
toggle_key = "F9"     # text goes in double quotes

[polymorph]
targets = ["dragon", "daemon"]   # a list: square brackets, quotes, commas[/code]

[size=4][b]1. Autocast[/b][/size]
Every spell has its own switch under [b][spells][/b]. On by default: Heal (only units missing 10+ hit points, most hurt first), Slow, Bloodlust on units that are fighting, and Raise Dead.
[list]
[*][b]Paladin:[/b] Heal, Exorcism on enemy undead, Holy Vision
[*][b]Mage:[/b] Slow, Polymorph (you list the valid targets, in priority order), Fireball, Blizzard, Flame Shield, Invisibility
[*][b]Ogre-Mage:[/b] Bloodlust, Runes, Eye of Kilrogg (an idle ogre-mage at full mana, and the eye flies to unexplored ground by itself)
[*][b]Death Knight:[/b] Raise Dead (any fresh corpse within 15 tiles, the computer's own rule), Death Coil, Haste, Unholy Armor, Whirlwind, Death and Decay
[/list]
Your move orders are never interrupted, two casters never pick the same target, and an eye you move yourself is left to you.

[b]Area spells hurt your own units in this game[/b], so the eight area and utility spells are off by default and, when you do switch them on, they are only cast with no friendly unit, building or wall in the blast area. A Blizzard or Death and Decay the mod started is stopped as soon as a friendly walks in. Both go for enemy buildings first (one building is enough, otherwise three units close together), aim at the middle of the building, and stop once the waves already cast cover its hit points.
[code][spells]
exorcism = true           # switch more spells on
polymorph = true
death_coil = true
fireball = true
blizzard = true

[heal]
min_missing_hp = 25       # only heal real wounds

[polymorph]
# the complete rule: only listed units are sheeped, earlier in the list wins
targets = ["dragon", "gryphon_rider", "daemon", "death_knight", "mage"]

[haste]
flyers_only = false       # haste every fighting unit, not just flyers

[eye_of_kilrogg]
cast = true
max_active = 3            # eyes out at once, all your ogre-magi together

[autocast]
area_min_enemies = 3      # enemy units needed close together when no enemy building is in the blast
area_building_value = 3   # Blizzard / Death and Decay: an enemy building counts as this many units
fireball_min_enemies = 2  # enemies needed along a Fireball's path[/code]

[size=4][b]2. Workers[/b][/size]
An idle peasant or peon repairs a damaged building of yours nearby (on by default), and with auto_harvest switched on a worker that is still idle walks to the nearest gold mine or tree. A worker carrying gold or lumber delivers it first. "Idle" means stopped with nothing queued; [b]Stand Ground[/b] is respected, so that is how you park a worker.
[code][workers]
auto_repair = true
repair_idle_seconds = 1
repair_radius = 10
auto_harvest = true
harvest_idle_seconds = 10
harvest_radius = 5[/code]

[size=4][b]3. Buildings that train by themselves[/b][/size]
[b][auto_production][/b] (off by default, Ctrl+F10 in game): every idle building of yours trains on its own, all of them at once. Halls, keeps and castles make workers, barracks, aviaries / roosts, mage towers / temples and shipyards make the army.

The mix is not a fixed list. The map decides how much of it is ships (the water fraction plus the oil patches on it), your hall tier decides the land mix, a unit line with upgrades gets a bigger share, and what you can actually afford bends the rest, so plenty of gold with little lumber quietly turns into grunts. It keeps food free for your own building (4 or 10 %, whichever is more), keeps back what your next upgrade costs, and obeys a mission's unit restrictions. It never touches the computer's buildings or one you have selected, never starts a research, and never builds transports, flying machines / zeppelins, sappers / dwarves, heroes or a second oil tanker.
[code][auto_production]
enabled = true
workers_tier1 = 12         # workers wanted with a town hall, a keep, a castle
workers_tier2 = 16
workers_tier3 = 24
navy_weight = 1.0          # how much of the map's water turns into ships (0 = never build ships)
bank_multiple = 4.0        # spare money needed before a unit: this many of its own price

[auto_production.land_tier3]
# weights, not percentages: only the ratios matter
knights = 40               # ogres / knights
casters = 25               # death knights / mages
archers = 15
flyers = 15
siege = 5
infantry = 0[/code]

[size=4][b]4. Gold, oil, food and forests[/b][/size]
[code][gold_mines]
unlimited = true          # off by default. Covers every mine, the computer's too
amount = 3.0              # or: three times the gold in every mine when a new map starts (1.0 = unchanged)

[oil_platforms]
unlimited = true          # off by default. Same thing for oil, its own switch
amount = 2.0              # x the oil in every patch and platform when a new map starts

[food]
hall_food = true          # off by default: halls, keeps and castles give food, so you can train before the first farm
hall_food_amount = 5      # the game's own value is 1, a farm gives 4

[trees]
regrow = true             # EXPERIMENTAL, off by default: felled forest grows back, so lumber never runs out for good
regrow_min_minutes = 10   # every stump waits its own time between min and max
regrow_max_minutes = 20
building_distance = 3     # never this close to a building or wall
unit_distance = 3         # never this close to a ground unit; it also never closes a passage[/code]

[size=4][b]5. Heroes and regeneration[/b][/size]
[code][heroes]
regen = true              # off by default
regen_hp_per_second = 2
regen_for = "all"         # or "mine"

[unit_regen]
enabled = true            # off by default: every unit and ship regenerates, never a structure
hp_per_second = 1
regen_for = "all"         # or "mine"[/code]
The [b]units[/b] list under [heroes] decides who counts as a hero, for regeneration and for the "heroes" health multiplier. All 15 campaign heroes are listed by default.

[size=4][b]6. Multipliers: health, prices, build and research time[/b][/size]
Three sections with the same layout: [b][health][/b], [b][costs][/b], [b][time][/b]. Everything is 1.0 (the game's own number) until you change it. 0.5 halves, 2.0 doubles. The values [b]multiply into each other from the top down[/b]:
[code]master "all"  x  master units / structures / research  x  race "all"  x  race units / structures / research  x  the group[/code]
[code][health]
all = 2.0               # EVERYTHING: every unit, ship and building has double health
units = 1.0             # or only units, leaving buildings alone
structures = 1.5        # every structure of both races

[health.orc]
all = 1.5               # orcs another 50 % on top
melee = 1.2             # grunts, ogres, ogre-magi 20 % on top of that

[health.human]
heroes = 4.0

[costs.human]
units = 0.5             # every human unit half price
naval_upgrades = 0.5    # ship cannon and ship armor research

[costs]
structures = 0.5        # every building and building upgrade half price

[time]
all = 0.5               # train, build and research twice as fast[/code]
[b]Masters:[/b] all, units, structures, research. [b]Groups:[/b] workers, melee, ranged, siege, casters, air, naval, demolition, heroes (health only), buildings, building_upgrades (keep, castle, guard and cannon tower), melee_upgrades, ranged_upgrades, siege_upgrades, naval_upgrades, paladin_upgrades / ogre_mage_upgrades, mage_spells / death_knight_spells. Prices scale gold, lumber and oil alike.
[b]Limits of the engine:[/b] 65535 hit points (32767 for buildings), 2550 for a unit or building price (steps of 10), 65535 for a research price, 255 for a build or research time.

[size=4][b]7. Spell damage, mana cost and regeneration[/b][/size]
Spells deal fixed damage, so once there is more health on the field they fall behind. [b][spell_damage][/b] multiplies the damage of every damage spell and the healing of Heal; [b][spell_cost][/b] multiplies every mana cost (rounded up). Both also take a number per spell that replaces the game's own first. [b][mana] regen[/b] makes casters refill faster; the 255 mana limit is part of the game and cannot be raised. These numbers are global, so the computer's casters get them too, and the game's own values come back in multiplayer.
[code][spell_damage]
all = 2.0            # every damage spell and Heal x2
death_coil = -1      # or set one spell's damage yourself (-1 = the game's)

[spell_cost]
all = 1.0
fireball = 50        # the game: 100
heal = 3             # Heal and Exorcism cost mana PER HIT POINT (the game: 5 and 4)

[mana]
regen = 1.5          # 50 % faster mana regeneration[/code]

[size=4][b]8. Your own numbers for a single unit or building[/b][/size]
One [b][unit.name][/b] table per unit, one [b][building.name][/b] table per building (same keys; damage and range matter for towers). [b]-1[/b], or leaving a line out, keeps the game's own number. 0 is a real value where it makes sense. These are base numbers: they go in first, and the multipliers above apply on top.
[code][unit.elven_destroyer]
hit_points = 105        # game: 100
armor = 11              # game: 10
basic_damage = 37       # game: 35
piercing_damage = 2     # game: 0
range = 5               # game: 4
sight = 9               # game: 8   (9 is the engine's maximum)
gold = 600              # game: 700
lumber = 300            # game: 350
oil = 500               # game: 700
build_time = 80         # game: 90[/code]
The file ships with this destroyer example, sight 8 for dragons and gryphon riders (the game's value is 6) and a blank template as [b]comments[/b]: remove the "# " in front of the lines to switch one on.
[code][range]
upgrade_bonus = 2       # range that Longbow / Lighter Axes add. The game's own bonus is 1[/code]

[size=4][b]When do changes apply?[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Right away:[/b] spells and their targets, workers, auto-production, unlimited mines and oil, hall food, tree regrowth, hero and unit regeneration, spell damage and mana cost, the hotkeys, the Longbow / Lighter Axes bonus.
[*][b]When a new map starts[/b] (new mission, custom game, restart): health, prices, build times, the richer mines and [unit.name] tables. A savegame keeps the numbers it was made with, so saves from before you installed the mod stay exactly as they were.
[*]The computer plays by the same numbers you do.
[/list]

[size=5][b]Good to know[/b][/size]
[list]
[*][b]Single-player only.[/b] What the mod does is local and would desync a network game, so it turns itself off there.
[*]Made for game build [b]1.0.2.2818[/b]. After a Blizzard patch the mod does nothing (the game runs normally) until it is updated. gameplay_options.log then says "staying inert".
[*]Battle.net "Scan and Repair" may remove version.dll. Just extract the zip again; your gameplay_options.toml is not touched.
[*][b]Nothing happens and there is no x86\gameplay_options.log after starting the game?[/b] Then the game you started is not the copy the mod is in. Check your shortcut: it must start Warcraft II.exe from the same x86 folder that holds version.dll (a second or older install elsewhere on the disk is the usual reason). Starting from the Battle.net app always runs the right one.
[*][b]Want to see what it is doing?[/b] Set log_casts = true under [general]. The log then writes a line for every spell cast, every unit auto-production starts, and a line saying why nothing was produced.
[/list]
